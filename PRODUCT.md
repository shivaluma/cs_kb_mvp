# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are CS agents working under live case-handling pressure. They need to find the correct SOP quickly, trust that it is the latest published version, copy approved macro responses, and identify escalation rules without asking a lead.

Secondary users are CS Leads, QA, SOP Admins, CS Ops, and Product/Tech Admins. They review and publish SOP versions, audit governance history, track usage analytics, identify knowledge gaps, and operate the search/AI pipeline.

## Product Purpose

CS SOP Knowledge Base centralizes SOP lookup, version governance, structured SOP content, search analytics, and AI-ready retrieval data. Success means agents stop relying on Excel/email, find SOPs faster, use only the latest approved version, and generate measurable search/adoption signals for CS Ops.

## Brand Personality

Operational, exact, calm. The interface should feel like a serious internal tool for high-volume support work: fast to scan, low ambiguity, and quietly trustworthy.

## Anti-references

Do not make it look like a marketing landing page, chatbot-first assistant, decorative AI dashboard, glassy SaaS hero, oversized metric board, or generic card grid. Avoid dark-mode-by-default, purple gradients, decorative blobs, and playful visuals that reduce confidence in policy accuracy.

## Design Principles

1. Put lookup speed before expression: search, filters, result confidence, and version status must stay immediately visible.
2. Make governance visible: every SOP view should expose published version, owner, updated date, and source/citation signals.
3. Keep AI subordinate to approved SOP content: AI suggestions should look helpful but constrained, always grounded with citations.
4. Design for repeated use: dense, predictable layouts are better than dramatic one-off compositions.
5. Surface friction: failed search, no-click behavior, stale SOPs, and feedback signals should become operational insight.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard navigation, focus states, and form controls. Support reduced motion. Avoid relying on color alone for status, confidence, or risk. Use clear labels that work for Vietnamese and English operational vocabulary.
